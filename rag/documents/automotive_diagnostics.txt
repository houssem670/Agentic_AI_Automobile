Title: Automotive Diagnostics - OBD-II and UDS
Domain: Automotive Diagnostics
Purpose: Support evidence-based diagnostic investigations

1. Diagnostic Overview
Automotive diagnostics help identify faults, retrieve diagnostic information
and guide troubleshooting of electronic control units (ECUs). Diagnostic
results must be interpreted in the context of the vehicle and its systems.

2. OBD-II
On-Board Diagnostics II (OBD-II) provides standardized diagnostic services
for emissions-related systems in supported vehicles. Diagnostic Trouble
Codes (DTCs) identify categories of detected faults. A DTC alone does not
always establish the underlying root cause.

3. Diagnostic Trouble Codes
A DTC should be interpreted using reliable documentation for the relevant
vehicle or system. Do not invent a meaning for an unknown code. Record the
code, its source, the conditions observed and any related evidence.

4. UDS
Unified Diagnostic Services (UDS), standardized in ISO 14229, defines
diagnostic services used to communicate with electronic control units.
Available services and access conditions depend on the ECU implementation.

5. Diagnostic Evidence
Useful evidence may include DTCs, timestamps, ECU responses, operating
conditions, communication errors and related CAN traffic. A network anomaly
does not by itself prove that an ECU has a physical or software fault.

6. Hypotheses and Root Cause
A probable cause is a hypothesis supported by available evidence.
A confirmed root cause requires sufficient verification. Diagnostic reports
should clearly separate observations, hypotheses and unknown information.

7. Recommended Investigation
- Preserve relevant logs and diagnostic responses.
- Consult documentation for the specific vehicle and ECU.
- Check whether the fault is reproducible under known conditions.
- Correlate DTCs with timestamps and related observations.
- Recommend safe, authorized checks before drawing conclusions.

8. Security and Safety
Diagnostic services can have security and safety implications. Testing
should use authorized systems and controlled environments. Do not send
diagnostic commands to a real vehicle without appropriate authorization
and validated procedures.

Source note:
General educational summary. Consult the applicable ISO 14229 edition,
