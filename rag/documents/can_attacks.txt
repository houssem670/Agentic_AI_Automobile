Title: Automotive CAN Attacks
Domain: Automotive Cybersecurity
Purpose: Support investigation of suspicious CAN traffic

1. Replay Attack
A replay attack involves recording legitimate messages and retransmitting
them later. Potential indicators include repeated payload sequences,
unexpected timing or messages appearing outside their normal context.
Repeated frames alone do not prove a replay attack.

2. Flooding Attack
A flooding attack generates an unusually high volume of messages and may
increase bus load or delay other communications. Detection can consider
message rates, identifier distribution and changes from a normal baseline.
High traffic may also result from legitimate operating conditions.

3. Spoofing Attack
Spoofing involves transmitting messages that imitate expected identifiers
or message content. An identifier by itself does not authenticate the
sender. Investigation may require timing analysis, payload validation
and additional trusted context.

4. Detection Principles
- Compare traffic with a known normal baseline.
- Examine identifiers, payloads, timestamps and message rates.
- Correlate independent indicators before assigning a risk level.
- Preserve raw observations and explain uncertainty.
- Distinguish a suspicious indicator from a confirmed attack.

5. Investigation Limitations
CAN traffic observations may not reveal the physical source of a message.
A detector can produce false positives, especially when normal traffic
patterns vary. Conclusions should reflect the available evidence.
