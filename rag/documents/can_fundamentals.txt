Title: Automotive CAN Bus Fundamentals
Domain: Automotive Networking
Purpose: Knowledge for CAN traffic analysis and cybersecurity investigation

1. Overview
The Controller Area Network (CAN) is a communication bus used by
electronic control units (ECUs) in vehicles. ECUs exchange messages
without requiring a central host for every communication.

2. CAN Frames
A CAN frame contains an arbitration identifier and a data field,
along with control and error-checking information. Classical CAN
supports up to 8 data bytes per frame. CAN FD supports larger payloads,
up to 64 data bytes.

3. CAN Identifiers
CAN identifiers participate in bus arbitration and help identify
messages. A lower numerical identifier generally has higher arbitration
priority. An identifier alone does not prove which ECU sent a message.

4. Periodic Traffic
Automotive ECUs often transmit messages periodically. Repeated messages
are therefore not automatically evidence of an attack. Analysis should
consider timing, identifier, payload, frequency and the expected context.

5. Traffic Monitoring
Useful observations include message counts, timestamps, inter-arrival
times, identifier distributions and payload changes. A baseline of
normal traffic helps identify unusual behavior.

6. Cybersecurity Limitations
An unexpected frequency or repeated payload is an indicator for
investigation, not definitive proof of malicious activity. Results
should record observed evidence, alternative explanations and unknowns.

7. Defensive Investigation
- Compare observed traffic with a known normal baseline.
- Inspect timestamps and payload sequences.
- Correlate multiple indicators before assigning a risk level.
- Preserve logs and document the limits of the analysis.

Source note:
General technical summary. Verify protocol details against authoritative
